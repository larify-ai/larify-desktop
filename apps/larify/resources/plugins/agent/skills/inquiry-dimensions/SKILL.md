The following questions define how to inquire into any subject recursively at any level of granularity, both epistemically and practically, in both directions, including competing alternatives.

# Epistemic Inquiry

- **Abstraction**
  - generalize: Inquire into the question "what does it generalize into, and why this generalization rather than another?"
  - specialize: Inquire into the question "what does it specialize into, and why this specialization rather than another?"
- **Composition**
  - decompose: Inquire into the question "what does it decompose into, and why this decomposition rather than another?"
  - compose: Inquire into the question "what does it compose into, and why this composition rather than another?"
- **Causation**
  - cause: Inquire into the question "what causes it, and why this cause rather than another?"
  - effect: Inquire into the question "what does it affect, and why this effect rather than another?"
- **Constraint**
  - prerequisite: Inquire into the question "what is its prerequisite, and why this prerequisite rather than another?"
  - dependent: Inquire into the question "what depends on it, and why this dependent rather than another?"

# Practical Inquiry

- **Uncertainty**
  - reduce: Inquire into the question "what uncertainty does it reduce, and why this reduction rather than another?"
  - increase: Inquire into the question "what uncertainty does it increase, and why this increase rather than another?"
- **Complexity**
  - reduce: Inquire into the question "what complexity does it reduce, and why this reduction rather than another?"
  - increase: Inquire into the question "what complexity does it increase, and why this increase rather than another?"
- **Cost / Risk**
  - reduce: Inquire into the question "what cost or risk does it reduce, and why this reduction rather than another?"
  - increase: Inquire into the question "what cost or risk does it increase, and why this increase rather than another?"

# Recursion Principle

Apply every inquiry recursively — for example, causes of causes, prerequisites of prerequisites, decompositions of decompositions, and further reductions in uncertainty, complexity, and cost / risk — until reaching first principles or further improvement is impossible.

# Prioritization

Prioritize tasks to maximize the rate of increasing epistemic breadth and depth and reducing epistemic opacity, while reducing practical uncertainty, complexity, and cost / risk.
